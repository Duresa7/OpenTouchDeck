# Idle screen-off

From M1-12 on, the firmware turns the backlight off after 600 seconds without a touch and clears every key modifier first, so nothing stays armed or locked on a dark screen. The first touch on the dark screen, real or injected, only wakes it. A gate between the touch stream and LVGL eats that contact from its first sample to the sample after the finger lifts, so no tile fires, no key goes out, and the page does not change. The next touch passes as before. The gate reads lit or dark from the CH422G output byte, the same byte the `backlight` command writes, so the two never disagree. A developer or the harness reads the gate's state and sets a shorter timeout over the debug console with `power`.

## Sub-features

- `idle-off` clears the backlight bit once the idle clock has run for the timeout with no contact open. The CH422G output byte goes from 30 to 26, `power: backlight off` logs, and `off_count` rises by 1. The check runs inside the LVGL read callback, once every 10 ms, so the screen goes dark between the timeout and the timeout plus about 0.1 s after the last sample with a finger down. Every sample with a finger down restarts the clock, so the screen never goes dark under a finger.
- `idle-clears-mods` calls `keys_clear_all` before the dark write, so every modifier reads `OFF` afterward. No report goes out for it.
- `idle-state` answers `power state` with `state` (`ACTIVE` when the backlight bit is set, `OFF` when it is clear), `idle_timeout_s`, `idle_ms` (since the last touching sample, or since the timeout was set if that is later), `swallowing` (true only while an eaten contact is open), `swallowed` (samples dropped since boot, the wake press and the closing release included), `off_count`, `wake_count`, `wake_us` (the latest wake, from the wake sample reaching the gate to the backlight write returning), `errors` (failed backlight writes), and `output` (the CH422G output byte).
- `idle-timeout-set` answers `power idle-timeout <seconds>` with `{"cmd":"power","ok":true,"op":"idle-timeout","idle_timeout_s":S}`. `S` is 1 to 86400. Setting it restarts the idle clock. It is not stored, so a reset restores 600. Setting it while the screen is dark keeps the screen dark.
- `wake-eaten` lights the screen on the first sample of a contact that starts dark. The output byte goes back to 30, `power: backlight on` logs, `wake_count` rises by 1, and `wake_us` holds the wake's time. Every sample of that contact is dropped, and so is the release that ends it, so `swallowed` rises by 2 for a tap and by `samples` for a swipe. LVGL takes its previous sample, released, instead, so it never sees a press appear or lose its release. `touch tap` and `touch swipe` still reply while the screen is dark.
- `wake-hold` eats a contact whole, however long it lasts, because its fate is fixed at its first sample. A 1500 ms hold that starts dark adds 152 to `swallowed`, clicks nothing, and leaves `swallowing` false once its release has passed. A contact whose first sample was lit keeps passing even if the backlight goes dark under it, so a press LVGL has seen always gets its release.
- `wake-next` passes the next contact after a wake unchanged, so a second tap on a tile sends its key.
- `wake-guard` eats a contact that starts with a real finger down within 250 ms of the closing sample of an eaten contact, whatever the screen shows. The controller can drop a finger for a few reads mid-touch, which reads as a lift and then a new press, and without the guard that second press would click the tile under the wake finger. An injected contact has no real finger and is never held back, so a second `touch tap` right behind the wake tap passes.
- `idle-backlight-cmd` keeps the M1-04 `backlight on|off` command working. After `backlight off`, the next touch is a wake and is eaten. `backlight on` lights a dark screen without eating anything and starts a new idle period.
- `idle-errors` answers `power` alone, `power state` with extra words, or an unknown subcommand with `usage: power state | idle-timeout <seconds 1-86400>`, a timeout word that is not plain digits with `usage: power idle-timeout <seconds 1-86400>`, and 0 or a number above 86400 with `out of range`. Each reply has `ok` false. Before the gate has started, which never happens after boot in practice, a well-formed command answers `power not ready`. A failed backlight write logs `power: backlight off write failed: <err>` or `power: backlight on write failed: <err>` as a warning and raises `errors`. A failed dark write leaves the screen lit, is followed at once by one lit write because a timed-out transfer may have landed, and is retried after 5 seconds. If that lit write fails too, `errors` rises by 2, the log adds `power: backlight on write after the failed off write failed too`, and the next contact is eaten and rewrites the byte, so a screen that may be dark under a byte that says lit never passes a touch. A failed wake write still eats its contact and is retried on a later sample of that contact after 250 ms, or on the first sample of the next contact.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Leave the deck alone for 10 minutes. The screen goes dark. Tap it once, and it lights without sending a key or changing the page. Tap a tile again, and the key goes out as usual.
- Open a serial terminal on the board's port and type `power state` or `power idle-timeout 5` at `td> `.
- Run `$TD serial cmd "<line>"`, which sends the same line and prints the reply as JSON. Run `$TD serial capture --no-reset --send "<line>"` when the `power:` log lines are the evidence.

## Driving it with touchdeck.ps1

Preconditions:

- The build under test is M1-12 or later, and `$TD serial doctor` exits `0` with `build_matches` true.
- Evidence directory `E=<absolute path>/.verify/<YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS>-idle` is new, and Doctor's JSON is saved there as `doctor.json`.
- From M1-12 on, run `$TD serial cmd "power state"` before any touch recipe, in this file or another. If `state` is `OFF`, wake the screen with one `touch tap 266 128`, the gap between F1 and F2 on page 0, or run `$TD serial cmd "power idle-timeout 86400"` for a long run and restore 600 afterward. A board left idle longer than the timeout is dark, and its first injected tap is eaten. At older links `power` is an unknown command, so the check costs one exit `1` and nothing else.
- Nobody touches the panel during these recipes. A real touch restarts the idle clock, wakes the screen, or clicks a tile.
- Each recipe starts lit, on page 0 at rest, with every modifier `OFF` and the record empty, unless it says otherwise. Run `$TD serial cmd "ui goto 0"`, `$TD serial cmd "keys clear all"`, and `$TD serial cmd "power state"`, and require `state` `ACTIVE` and `output` 30.
- Every `$TD` call starts pwsh and activates ESP-IDF before it opens the port. On the dev PC a `serial cmd` call took about 2 s, a full-size screenshot or a `keys` dump a few seconds more, and an `idf` call about 5 s. Two separate `cmd` calls therefore land about 2 s apart. No recipe here relies on two calls landing within 8 s of each other, so a slow call cannot break one. Taps that must land close together go in one session, with `--repeat N --interval S` on `cmd` or `--send ... --send-times N --send-every S` on `capture`.
- "Darken the screen" below means this sequence. Run `$TD serial cmd "power idle-timeout 5"`, then `Start-Sleep -Seconds 7`, then `$TD serial cmd "power state" > $E/<name>-off.json`, and require `state` `OFF` and `output` 26. Then run `$TD serial cmd "power idle-timeout 60"`. Its reply is `{"cmd":"power","ok":true,"op":"idle-timeout","idle_timeout_s":60}`, and the screen stays dark. The 60 gives the steps that follow room, because the wake restarts the idle clock and each later call lands a few seconds after the one before.
- Every recipe ends with `$TD serial cmd "power idle-timeout 600"`, unless it reboots, because a reset restores 600 on its own.
- Tile aims come from `ui tile <id>`. At the M1-06 theme, `ui tile f5` gives `page` 0, `x` 28, `y` 196, `w` 230, `h` 104, `cx` 143, and `cy` 248, so F5 is `touch tap 143 248`, and `ui tile ctrl` gives `x` 274, `y` 436, `cx` 389, and `cy` 488. Those follow from the grid in [UI pages](./ui-pages.md), and the M1-12 owner read the same boxes from `ui tile` on the board. The gap between F1 and F2 is `266 128` on page 0, from the boxes of `ui tile f1` and `ui tile f2`. F5 is usage 62.
- Pixel reader, run once per PowerShell session, as in [UI pages](./ui-pages.md): `function px($f, $w, $x, $y) { $b = [IO.File]::ReadAllBytes($f); $o = ($y * $w + $x) * 2; $b[$o] + 256 * $b[$o + 1] }`. The tokens read here are `tile` 6406, `bg` 2114, `tile_down` 10765, `glow` 19743, and the locked modifier fill `mod_locked` 8984, which is `UI_RGB565(32, 96, 192)`.

## Gotchas

- Every `$TD` call starts pwsh and activates ESP-IDF before it opens the port, which took about 2 s for a `serial cmd` call and about 5 s for an `idf` call on the dev PC. Two separate `cmd` calls land about 2 s apart, so never write a recipe where two calls must land within about 8 s of each other. A `power idle-timeout 5` followed by a separate call that must still find the screen lit leaves a margin of 2 to 3 s, which a slow call can overrun. Use a timeout of 15 or more when the screen must stay lit across calls, and keep taps that must land close together inside one session with `--repeat` and `--interval`, or one `capture` with `--send-times` and `--send-every`.
- To make the screen dark on purpose, set `power idle-timeout 5`, wait 7 s, and require `OFF` with `output` 26 from `power state`. Then set `power idle-timeout 60`. Setting the timeout restarts the idle clock and keeps a dark screen dark, and the 60 covers the calls that follow the wake.
- From M1-12 on, a board left idle longer than the timeout is dark, and the first injected tap, swipe, or hold is eaten whole. That includes the regression lanes that run M1-10 and M1-11 recipes at this head. Read `power state` first, wake the screen with a tap in the gap at `266 128`, or set `power idle-timeout 86400` for a long run and restore 600 afterward.
- `power idle-timeout` is not stored. A reset restores 600, and nothing else does, so every recipe that changes it ends with `power idle-timeout 600`. A recipe left at 5 darkens the board for the next run.
- Nobody may touch the panel during these recipes. A finger restarts the idle clock, wakes a dark screen and is eaten, or clicks a tile.
- A screenshot taken while the screen is dark still streams and still shows the deck, because LVGL keeps rendering into the frame buffer and only the backlight is off. It proves nothing about the backlight. Only `state` and `output` from `power state`, or `output` from `backlight`, say what the chip was told, and only a person at the deck can see whether the panel is dark. Report the physical wake as pending operator and point to `.claude/docs/touchdeck-physical-tests.md`.
- The idle check runs in the LVGL read callback, once every 10 ms, and a frame takes up to about 65 ms while LVGL draws. The screen goes dark between the timeout and the timeout plus about 0.1 s after the last sample with a finger down, never on the exact second.
- `touch tap` on a dark screen takes a little longer to reply than on a lit one, because the eaten press and its release are taken in two reads where a passed tap has both in one. The M1-12 owner read `deliver_us` 9700 to 13200 for eaten taps and 5000 to 9300 for passed ones, so one run shows no clean difference, and both sit far under the 60000 bound.
- `swallowing` is true only while an eaten contact is open, from the wake press to the sample after its release. By the time a `touch tap` or `touch swipe` has replied it reads false. To catch it true, put `ui hold` and `power state` as two `--send` lines in one `capture`, because `ui hold` replies while the hold runs on.
- An eaten `ui hold` replies after about 0.15 s with an empty `pressed_tile`, where a passed hold replies after about 0.2 s with the tile's name. That is the wait for a press that never registers.
- `touch stats` counts an eaten injected sample in `injected` as well, because the sample left the queue before the gate ate it. `queued` is 0 once the gesture has drained.
- `swallowed` counts the wake press and the closing release. A tap on the dark screen adds 2, a swipe or a hold adds its `samples`, which is `ms` divided by 10 plus 2.
- `backlight off` darkens the screen without the gate, so `off_count` does not move, and the next touch is still a wake. `backlight on` lights a dark screen without eating anything and starts a new idle period. A `backlight on` while the screen is already lit changes nothing, the idle clock included.
- `power: backlight on` and `power: backlight off` go through stdio, which the display can drop while it redraws. `off_count`, `wake_count`, and `swallowed` in `power state` are the proof when a count matters.
- The screen-off clears every modifier before the dark write, and the modifier tiles redraw from the engine state on a 50 ms poll, so a locked Ctrl is `OFF` in `keys state` and plain on the tile while the screen is still dark. A lock set more than the timeout before its key is gone.
- `errors` above 0, or a `power: backlight ... write failed` warning, is a finding. A failed dark write leaves the screen lit and is retried after 5 s, and a failed wake write still eats its contact and is retried after 250 ms on a later sample of that contact, or on the first sample of the next contact.
- A real finger that lands within 250 ms of the end of an eaten contact is eaten too, and an injected contact never is. A recipe that follows a real wake with a real tap has to wait longer than that. The recipes here use injected taps, so the guard never touches them.
- `help` lists `power` after `keys`, twelve names in all. Take the argument forms from this file. `help` only confirms the name.
- The `t` field of a record entry or `mod` reply and the `I (ms)` stamp on a log line are two clocks. They differ by a constant that changes each boot, 292 ms on one M1-12 run. Compare times within one clock only. To line a record entry up with a log line, measure the offset on that boot first.
- The board lock. Only the holder of `.verify/hardware.lock` opens the port. The idle clock, the timeout, and the counters are one set for the whole board, so another run's tap wakes your dark screen.
